I’ve had three grandfathers, 

One died before I was born, 
I learned he was kind, generous, and optimistic.  
An analytical thinker, but a social creature. 
My eldest brother is named for him, and that says a lot. 
I am proud of him never truly knowing his company.


My other grandfather died when I was a teenager,
He was incredibly sharp, with a mind for numbers and a love for monopoly. 

I saw the joy he took from his own acumen,
yet he carried himself in a quiet, principled, and humble way.
Always measured, his last words to me were of safety.

But how do I have three? 

Well, my maternal grandmother met a man when I was very young.
I was a page boy at their wedding. What was then cute, became something I am grateful for.
Intelligent, generous, funny, and grounded in a way I couldn’t until later comprehend.
He became a mentor: I learned to fish, play soccer, ride my bike better… 
All the while I learned to think, from a sarcastical, skeptical, curious man that I called Steve.
Over years, I took many problems I was facing to him for perspective. I always walked away a better person. 


As I shared my growing struggles, I learned of his past experiences. 
Unimaginable adversaries, cruel hardships. Yet he mixed them with humour. 
That kept me listening, learning, and idolising a soul I hope to live up to. 


I miss all my grandfathers.

But one changed me most, and for the better.
